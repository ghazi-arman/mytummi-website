At one point you've probably experienced bloating, indegestion, or nauseau at the worst possible time. Whether that was during a meeting, before an outing with friends, or even in the middle of a meal. 


But for the majority of people these issues aren't frequent or severe enough to consult a dietician or doctor.


Throughout my life I've dealt with stomach issues that were annoying but managable so I never prioritized fixing them.

However there was one month where my bloating and indegistion became so bad I finally caved. I set out to find the easiest way I could get to the root cause of my stomach problems.

## Where the idea came from

Most online resources tell you the best way to identify your food sensitivities is to keep a food/symptom journal and try elimination diets.

I was already logging the meals I ate in my fitness tracker and appreciated how easy it was using their search feature and barcode scanner.

I was also using Yuka to see the additive and nutrition scores of products I eat.

I thought to myself why not create an app that allows me to

1. Log my meals with ease
2. Tracks my calories and macros
3. Rates products but the way I want them to be scored
4. Use my journal to find what I might be sensitive to
5. Check products if they have ingredients I'm avoiding


I knew exactly what I wanted to build and spent the next few months doing it.

## What changed from the start

### Making food logging take seconds

When I was getting feedback from the My Tummi's first users, one common complaint was it wasn't easy enough to log what they ate.

As someone who has used apps like MyFitnessPal for years scanning barcodes, searching for food, and creating recipes were second nature to me.

But doing so still takes a decent amount of time and effort.

I decided to add a feature where users can describe their meal or just take a picture of their food.

This means logging a meal take seconds but our system still breaks down the food into the ingredients and categories we need for our analysis.


### Finding your sensitivities

Another major shift from the beggining was how we identified sensitivities.

At first, I was using AI (Claude) to identify patterns between the foods a user ate and any symptoms they have.

But anyone who's used AI before knows the mistakes it makes as well as how it's answer can change when you ask it the same question.

Instead I created a model that is more structured and understands what food categories cause which symptoms and how long it takes for them to show up.

I won't bore you with the details of the model here but I will write a future article on the specifics.

## How My Tummi helped me

As the first user of My Tummi I was essentially the company's guinea pig.

It took a couple weeks to identify my first sensitivities (capsaicin and sugar alcohol) but it was worth the time to understand my body.

My Tummi not only identifies potential sensitivities but sets up experiments to eliminate then reintroduce those triggers to see if they are actually causing your symptoms.

After running an experiment for each it became clear that both capsaicin and sugar alcohols were causing my bloating and indigestion.

Now I'm not going to say I cut out spicy foods completely since I couldn't live without them. But knowing that it may cause a reaction ensures I avoid them at certain times.

Which is exactly what I set out to do when building My Tummi.

It does take some level of commitment to log what you eat and how you feel but we made that as easy as possible. Try it out and you see what you can discover about your body!
